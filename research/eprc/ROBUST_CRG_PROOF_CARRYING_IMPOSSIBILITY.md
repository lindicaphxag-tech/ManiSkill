# Robust proof-carrying impossibility witness

Nominal CRG can prove that a target lies outside the estimated repairability
set. Robust CRG must prove more: the target must remain outside *every*
repairability set generated by an admissible local physical map.

Assume

    ||G_true - G_hat||_2 <= epsilon_G

and

    K_true = {G_true xi : ||xi||_2 <= r}.

For any unit physical direction n,

    sup_{y in K_true} n^T y
    <= r (||G_hat^T n||_2 + epsilon_G).

Therefore the compact witness

    n^T d - r (||G_hat^T n|| + epsilon_G) > tau

proves that no admissible local map can place the requested correction within
residual tolerance tau.

## Why it is useful

The independent verifier does not invoke the CRG optimizer and does not trust
its nearest-point implementation. It recomputes the robust support bound from:

- frozen G_hat;
- operator-norm uncertainty epsilon_G;
- certified radius r;
- target d;
- witness normal n;
- residual tolerance tau.

A positive margin is therefore a proof-carrying REJECT reason under the stated
uncertainty model.

## Novelty discipline

Robust support-function separation is established convex analysis. It is not
claimed as a new theorem. The research question is whether black-box
intervention-identified repair sets are stable and predictive enough that these
compact certificates remain useful on real frozen-policy deployment failures.
